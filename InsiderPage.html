What is an Insider Threat?

An insider threat is a threat that arises from any person who has or had authorised access to knowledge ofa comapanies resources, including personell, facilities, networks and systems, and has the potential to harm an organisation. According to a verizon data breach report, it was said that 20% of breaches were caused by actors within an organisation. 1


Types of Insider Breaches:


Unintentional: Negligence

An insider that exposes an organization to a cyber threat out of carelessness or ignorance...

Unintentional: Accidental

Accidental threats can include mistyping an email address, sending confidential documents to the wrong recipient...

Intentional: Malicious Insider

Insiders who deliberately harm an organization for personal gain, revenge, or financial incentives...

Intentional: Third-Party

These are individuals granted access to company systems but misuse it for cyber espionage...



How to identify an Insider?

There are plenty of tools and measures in place that can help companies identify potential insiders:
- A robust implementation of authentication controls and Insider Risk Management Tools such as Teramind which monitors employee activity and efficiency and offers functions that block, alert and redirect suspicious behaviour with real time access to user activities. 2
- Downloading or accessing unnatural amounts of data not related to their job.
- Asking for access to tools or resources not needed for their job.
- Using unauthorized external storage devices like USBs, truning off encryption, attempting to bypass access controls.
- Network crawling accompanied by sensitive data searches
- Hoarding data and copying files from sensitive folders.
- Logging in outside of usual hours.

How to prevent Insider Attacks?

The criticial steps to mitigate insider threats are Define, Detect and Identify, Assess, and Manage.
1. Detect: Organizations must quickly spot suspicious or unusual activity on their networks. This includes monitoring user logins, tracking locations, and receiving alerts about any anomalies. Strong security tools provide real-time visibility and help identify threats before they escalate.
2. Investigate Detecting suspicious activity isn't enough—organizations must act fast. Investigating immediately helps stop attackers before they gain access to sensitive systems or escalate their attack.
3. Prevent Once a threat is identified, organizations must block unauthorized access to their systems. Tools like VPNs help encrypt data and keep user activity private, reducing insider threats.
4. Protect Security policies should be clear, and employees must understand their data access and privacy responsibilities. Protecting critical assets—such as technology, intellectual property, and customer data—ensures compliance with strict data regulations.
